### The situation

We have observed that students in Grades 4 and 5 are developing stronger critical-thinking skills and a more nuanced understanding of social dynamics. At home, you may notice them exploring different digital tools and conducting online research for school projects with growing independence and confidence.

### The problem

Children are often exposed to questionable information, from social-media feeds and news headlines to viral videos and AI-generated search results, that can look convincingly like credible facts. Without the right skills, it can be difficult to tell the difference.

### The need

Students need strong information-literacy, media-literacy, and news-literacy skills to distinguish fact from fiction and make sense of the content they encounter every day.

### The solution

This workshop introduced students in Grades 4 and 5 to the SIFT method—a practical, four-step strategy for separating online “information gold” from “fool’s gold.” It was designed to help students develop the critical-thinking skills needed to navigate online sources with discernment and make informed decisions about what to believe and share.

### Workshop objectives

By the end of the workshop, students were able to:
- Understand the difference between fact and fiction.
- Practise finding facts and spotting fiction in the media.
- Identify credible sources of information using the SIFT method.
- Spot AI-generated content by noticing visual clues, such as text errors and unusual details, and by drawing on prior knowledge.
- Think critically about digital images and learn to verify what is authentic and what has been altered.
- Explain their reasoning through discussion, practising both quick decision-making and thoughtful analysis.

### SIFT

The SIFT method is an evaluation strategy developed by digital-literacy expert Mike Caulfield to help determine whether online content is credible and reliable. The information about SIFT on this page is adapted from his materials under a CC BY 4.0 licence.

Determining whether online resources are credible can be challenging. Use the SIFT method to help evaluate information, especially news and other online media.
